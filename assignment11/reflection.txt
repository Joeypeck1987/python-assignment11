Task 4: Reflection

1. Static and interactive visualizations

Static visualizations show a fixed view of the data and work well in printed reports or presentations. 
The employee revenue bar chart and cumulative revenue line chart made comparisons and trends easy to see without needing to interact with them. 
The interactive visualizations let the viewer explore the data by hovering over points, zooming, or hiding categories. 
The Plotly wind chart showed how these features can provide more detail while keeping the chart readable. 
Best choice depends on the audience and whether they need a quick overview or a chance to explore.

2. Dashboards for real-time data exploration

Dashboards bring charts, tables, and key measurements together in one place, making it easier to monitor changes and explore data.
The filters let users focus on a particular products, time periods, or groups without creating a new report each time. 
When a dashboard is connected to a current data source and configured to refresh, it can help users spot trends or problems sooner and make informed decisions. 
Its usefulness depends on accurate data and a clear indication of when the information was last updated.

3. Interactive tools in professional settings

Tools like Plotly improve data communication by letting people explore the details behind a chart. 
Hover information can show exact values, and zooming can help viewers examine a specific area. 
Clicking legend items makes it easier to compare selected groups. 
These features can support discussions in meetings because people can investigate questions as they arise. 
Clear titles, labels, and explanations still matter so that everyone understands what the chart represents. 
Saving a Plotly chart as HTML also makes it possible to share an interactive visualization that others can open in a browser.
